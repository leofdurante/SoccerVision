The counter-intuitive one

ByteTrack’s fuse_score had to be turned off. Folding confidence into the match cost penalises exactly the weak-but-real detections that matter at this resolution — it was costing a third of all tracked detections.

Error analysis

Three failures the metrics never showed

01
The minute we analysed was the broadcast intro A fixed 300-frame budget at 5 fps always read the first 60 seconds. On a 103-minute upload that is 0.88% of the match — title cards and a highlight montage. The “30 players tracked” were people in promo clips.
02
Our longest-lived player was the scorebug 197 frames, the most consistent track in the analysis — against a median of 12 for real players. No single signal caught it: a distant player sits just as high in frame, and a keeper stands just as still. Long-lived AND high AND static isolated exactly one track out of 181.
03
The pitch is an American football field White yard lines dominate every frame and belong to the wrong sport. The soccer lines are painted yellow — but so are the goalposts, one team’s kit, and the broadcast graphics.

So we labelled the metrics camera-relative, not pitch-relative. We prototyped two pitch-registration approaches and rejected both with measurements — chained motion compensation drifts ~400 px in a 640 px frame, and direct-to-reference loses overlap in 8 seconds. We did not ship coordinates we could not defend.

Where it goes

What we’d build next

Appearance-based re-ID IoU association is the ceiling we hit. 180 identities for 22 players means embeddings, not geometry — the exact bullet in the posting.
Pitch registration The yard lines that break line-detection are regularly spaced and numbered. They could give drift-free absolute position instead.
A fixed-camera path Every commercial system requires one. With a static wide camera the homography is a one-time four-corner calibration.
What we actually practised

The posting asks for hypotheses, ablations, error analysis and measurable improvements. Every number in this deck came out of that loop — including the two approaches we threw away.
